# swift-typesetting

Use this package when you set a string in a font at a size and need the result as values: glyph outlines at places in lines, and the measures of the font and of the lines (ascent, descent, leading, cap height, x-height, line widths, the box of the ink). Start from `Typesetting.Text(_:font:size:measure:alignment:leading:tracking:figures:)` with a font parsed by swift-iso-14496-22, or from the font's bytes with `init(_:bytes:...)`. A `Typesetting.Text` holds `lines` of `Typesetting.Glyph`s, each with its outline as a `Typesetting.Plane.Path` of swift-geometry, and its `metrics`. `circled(center:radius:start:side:anchor:level:)` and `along(_:tolerance:anchor:level:)` set the glyphs on a circle or a path. The package draws nothing: swift-picture turns a text into picture elements, and any other renderer takes the paths. Reading a font is swift-iso-14496-22's; line alignment is swift-layout's `Horizontal.Alignment` and its `indent(of:in:direction:)`, left to right.

## Laws

- A text is set in its own plane, `Typesetting.Plane`, swift-geometry's with y up, as the font has it: the first line's baseline is y 0, each later line one `leading` lower, and x 0 is the left of the measure. A renderer whose y points down negates y.
- Every code point but U+000A becomes exactly one glyph, in the order of the string, through the font's character map; a code point the font does not map becomes glyph 0. There is no shaping: no ligatures, no combining, no reordering, no fallback font, and left to right only, so `leading` alignment is the left. The one adjustment between glyphs is pair kerning, `ISO_14496_22.FontFile.kerning(left:right:)`. `Typesetting.Text.length(of:)` counts the glyphs a string is set as, one for each scalar that is not U+000A, so a string's glyphs are the next that many of a text it is part of.
- The pen starts each line at x 0 and moves by each glyph's advance width plus the kerning with the glyph after it on the same line, all times `size` over the font's units per em, and by `tracking` times `size`: tracking is a share of the em, so it scales with the text. A glyph's `origin` is where the pen stood, on its line's baseline; its `advance` is its own advance width at that size, without kerning or tracking. A line of n glyphs is so (n − 1) times the tracking wider, also where it breaks at a measure, which counts the tracking.
- A glyph's `outline` is the font's outline scaled by `size` over units per em around the glyph's own origin, y up; `path` is the outline moved to the origin, and `Typesetting.Text.path` is every glyph's path in order. `bounds` is the bounding box of that path, nil for a text that draws nothing, as one of spaces.
- With `figures: .tabular` each digit U+0030 to U+0039 is set on `Metrics.figure`, the widest of the ten digits' advances, its outline moved right by half of what that leaves over its own advance, and no kerning applies on either side of a digit, so a number's width depends only on how many digits it has. swift-iso-14496-22 reads no `GSUB`, so this is a layout of the font's own digits, not its `tnum` feature. With `figures: .cells(_:)` each digit is set as `.tabular` sets it and each other character in the share of a figure cell its class is given by `Typesetting.Figures.Cells`: `punctuation` (Unicode's P categories), `symbols` (S) and `spaces` (Zs), its outline centred there and no kerning on either side; a class given no share keeps its own advance, so `.tabular` is `.cells(Cells())`, bit for bit.
- `Typesetting.Metrics(font:size:)` is what a font measures at a size: `ascent`, `descent` and `gap` are the `hhea` ascender, minus its descender, and its line gap; `cap` and `ex` are the font's `sCapHeight` and `sxHeight` as swift-iso-14496-22 gives them (the `OS/2` value, or the top of H and x), and with neither the cap height is the ascent and the x-height the cap height. `figure` is the widest of the digits' advances, and `pitch` the advance of the space (or glyph 0) when the font `isMonospaced`, nil otherwise. A text's `metrics` are its font's at its size; its `leading` is the one given, or ascent plus descent plus gap.
- With a `measure`, a line takes as many words as fit: it breaks before a word that follows a U+0020 when the line up to the end of that word would be wider than the measure. The spaces at the break stay at the end of the line as glyphs and do not count toward its `width`. A word wider than the measure stands alone on its line and is not broken. Without a measure a line ends only at U+000A, and the measure is the widest line's width. Each line is moved right by `alignment.indent(of:in:direction:)` of its width in the measure, left to right: nothing for `leading`, half of what it leaves of the measure for `center`, all of it for `trailing`.
- A text's `width` is its widest line's. Every advance, kerning and tracking is the size times a constant, so the width is too, and `size(fitting:maximum:)` is the text's size times the measure over its width, at most `maximum`: set again at that size, the widest line is the measure up to rounding. It takes the lines as they are set, so a text broken at a measure may break otherwise at the new size; a text with no width gives `maximum`.
- `point(at:level:)` is the point a text is put at or turned about: x is 0, half the measure or the measure for `leading`, `center` and `trailing`, and y is the `Typesetting.Level`'s `height(in:)` the metrics: 0 for the baseline, the ascent, minus the descent, or a share of `cap` or `ex`.
- `Typesetting.Glyph.transformed(by:)` takes a glyph's origin through an affine map and its outline through the map's linear part, so its path is its old path under the map. `circled(center:radius:start:side:anchor:level:)` turns every glyph so, without bending it: the middle of its advance on its baseline, at its distance along its line from `point(at:level:)`, goes that arc length along the circle from the angle `start`, counterclockwise from x. `outside` runs clockwise, its tops away from the centre, so it reads across the top; `inside` runs counterclockwise, its tops toward the centre, and reads across the bottom. The circle passes through the first line's level; a later line keeps the angles on a circle nearer the centre for `outside` and farther for `inside`. A radius that is not positive and finite gives no glyphs.
- `along(_:tolerance:anchor:level:)` does the same by length along a path flattened to within the tolerance, its subpaths one track and a closed one with its closing line, straight on before its start and past its end, each glyph's top a quarter turn counterclockwise from the way the path runs, so a path toward x carries the text upright and a counterclockwise circle carries it as `inside` does. A path with no length gives no glyphs.
- `Typesetting.Error` is `size` or `measure` for one that is not positive and finite, `tracking` for one that is not finite, `units` for a font whose em has no units, `font` with the font package's parsing error for bytes that are not a font or kerning data that is damaged, and `outline` with its outline error for a glyph that cannot be read.
